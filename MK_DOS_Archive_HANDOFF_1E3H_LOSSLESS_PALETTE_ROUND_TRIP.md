## 1E.3H question and implementation

Can every structurally qualified NEWPALS record be represented as typed fields, persisted through JSON, and serialized back without changing any original byte?

The new codec:

1. Parses sequential little-endian `[u16 count][u16 color words...]` records over the exact qualified module range.
2. Retains ordinal, relative offset, declared length, and every raw 16-bit word.
3. Retains duplicate-content records at their distinct addresses; no merging or reordering.
4. Keeps symbol aliases and original record coordinates as ground-truth annotations.
5. Regenerates every header and color word from the JSON-deserialized IR. The serializer does not copy original byte spans or derive words from display RGB.
6. Compares regenerated bytes with the original module, reinserts them into an in-memory executable copy, and compares every executable byte and the complete SHA-256.

The codec accepts lengths 2..255, matching the currently qualified grammar. It preserves all u16 word bits rather than masking them; authoritative execution is additionally constrained by the existing ground-truth prerequisite.

No-edit module serialization is the only established bidirectional capability. Resizing, relocation, ownership-table editing, executable patch export, and edited-game runtime validation remain future work.

## Provenance and failure handling

- The production pass rejects synthetic sources and failed prerequisites.
- Ground truth and transfer reports must refer to the same executable path, size, hash, and data-image base.
- The executable is read and SHA-256 is recomputed before using prerequisite offsets; changed source bytes fail closed.
- A new `palette_round_trip-...` operation ID is used and checked at export.
- Export reserializes the supplied IR and recomputes module byte/hash checks; cached pass flags alone are insufficient.
- Export includes original module hex, typed IR, original ground-truth words and aliases, source/rebuilt hashes, and mismatch counts for independent review.
- Whole-executable comparison is performed during execution. The JSON contains its result and hashes, not the entire executable; independent whole-file review still requires the original fixture.

Seven export mutation probes were rejected: changed IR word, operation ID, synthetic flag, prerequisite hash, rebuilt executable hash, record coordinate, and original evidence byte. An eighth probe changed the freshly read executable and was rejected by the recomputed SHA-256 check.
